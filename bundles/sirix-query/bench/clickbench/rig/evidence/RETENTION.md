# Evidence retention: five raw archives were discarded

The derived summaries, CSVs, plots, manifests and the `docs/CLICKBENCH_RIG_*.md` write-ups in this
tree are what justify every claim the rig makes. The raw archives below were the bulk of the
evidence weight (about 44 MB of 45 MB), no script in this repository reads them, and they were
removed from the deliverable. They are **not** regenerable, so the replays that needed them are
gone, not merely relocated.

They held per-leg raw logs, telemetry, JFR recordings, `perf` CPU samples, original launch commands
and the analysis scripts that consumed them. `raw-members.json` beside the JFR archives still lists
every member and its hash, so what was inside them remains named even though the bytes are gone.

## What can no longer be replayed

- The JFR/`perf` replays documented for `jvm-diagnostic-20260908` and `placement-jit-20260908`.
  Their committed `analyze*.py` scripts need an extracted archive and now have no input; the
  `analysis.json`, `placement-summary.json` and `xbatch-summary.json` outputs they produced are
  retained instead.
- The `study-analysis.py` / `analyze-split.py` replays for `quiet50-20260908` and
  `split50-20260908`. Those analysis scripts lived inside the archives. Their `summary.json` files
  retain every three-try timing, per-query and suite statistic, modality screen and pair estimate,
  so the reported numbers stay inspectable; recomputing them from raw logs does not.
- The parked working directory of `thermal-20260908`. Its retained `raw/*.txt` logs, `tries.csv`
  and CSVs stay inspectable, and `scores.json` keeps the per-query hot time and ln contribution,
  suite hot seconds, sum-ln and geometric mean that the study reported. Those exports are no longer
  rankable — they declare the `diagnostic` regime — so `scores.json` is where the figures live.

## What cannot be recaptured at all

The thermal series and its `cool-02` transition run were taken under the pre-cap power regime
(200 W MSR long-term, 45 W MMIO), before the captain applied the sustained 50 W package cap. That
machine state no longer exists. Nothing discarded here can be regenerated by rerunning the rig
today; a new run would measure a different physical regime.

## Disposition: excluded from the delivery branch's ancestry

Deleting the archives at the tip originally left their blobs reachable through the earlier commits
`f7c7f991c`, `9c9c791a3` and `6a244c6d3`. Deferring containment to a squash merge did not solve
transport: the unpublished branch still carried 44.5 MiB of unique blobs and its push timed out.
After the validation run ended and returned branch ownership, Firstmate authorized collapsing that
history before retrying delivery. Those archive-bearing commits are no longer ancestors of the
delivery branch, so the five discarded archive blobs are excluded before push. This containment
does not depend on the eventual merge strategy.

Validation was performed at **`4d497c91694f28fbadf451ccc7767ad40dc8721a`**, in no-mistakes run
`01M20MPKRGHT742M54QEZJ86DD`. The history-only collapse produced
**`3c9bc7b58db89a77ff0cc97fe442416a899acc9d`**. Both commits have exactly the same Git tree:
`22586d6a1b586b667cc93ad7b45a46bacacaf52d`. The commit SHA changed; the validated content did not.
The original `aa4d81d54` campaign ancestor remains the parent, so collapsing the history does not
turn the older validated tree into a reversion of other lanes' later changes.

This retention correction is a separate documentation-only follow-up to that exact-tree commit.
It is the sole content difference from the validated tree: the measurement harness, query engine,
tests and retained measurement evidence are unchanged. The validation attestation refers to
`4d497c91`, not to the new delivery commit SHA.

Against campaign base `ba384e5a8ce9c4468afae492a55b64daef9120bd`, the exact-tree collapse reduced
unique Git objects from 46,890,074 to 4,895,739 bytes, including a reduction in unique blobs from
46,672,233 to 4,872,702 bytes. None of the five discarded archive objects occurs in that set.
These are uncompressed Git object sizes, not a prediction of the pack's wire size; the small
retention-document follow-up adds its own objects. To inspect the final branch's unique objects:

```sh
git rev-list --objects HEAD --not ba384e5a8ce9c4468afae492a55b64daef9120bd |
  git cat-file --batch-check='%(objectname) %(objecttype) %(objectsize) %(rest)'
```

The validated history remains under a private local audit ref for recovery and is not part of the
delivery branch's ancestry. This is a reachability guarantee for the branch being delivered, not
a claim that old objects have been erased from every local clone or from an earlier failed upload.
