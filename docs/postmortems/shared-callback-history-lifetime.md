# Bug: shared callbacks outlived readers and history

- Fix / issue: review-callback-scope and review-truncate-view, following R16.
- Symptom: closing reader-owner session A could retire cursors still used by a notification dispatched by B; truncation could remove pages still visible to a revision-pinned post-hook. Final-session standalone notifications ran after closure and could not open permitted independent readers.
- Trigger: two user sessions share revision listeners; pause a listener borrowing A's direct/shared/page reader while B publishes and A closes. Separately pause a timed writer's revision-2 hook before distant-page navigation and call truncateTo(1).
- Root cause: callback completion belonged to a node dispatcher rather than the resource that shares listener admission. Reader retirement and destructive history changes had no shared admission-versus-retirement boundary. Waiting for hardening alone did not establish hook completion, and releasing T during a callback fence allowed another hardening epoch to arrive.
- Why tests missed it: writer and reader belonged to the same session, the final-session oracle checked lifecycle rejection but no permitted read, and the lineage oracle registered no post-hook.
- Regression test: SharedCallbackLifetimeTest covers JSON/XML, node/immediate/maintenance publications, initial/late direct/shared/page readers, interruption, first/suppressed independent failures, admission during retirement, and revision-pinned distant-page reads versus truncation. TransactionExecutionLockTest.retirementRechecksWorkersAdmittedWhileCallbackDispatchReleasedExecution exercises a new epoch arriving during the fence. The earlier final-session test now opens and navigates an independent reader while the session remains open. Focused execution and limitations are recorded in the resource-callback verification artifact; the pre-fix source was not executed in this round.
- Lesson: match completion ownership to callback admission scope and fence both readers and the history they read; see [the lesson](../LESSONS.md).

Lock order: writer admission waits for P outside S, then acquires a short notification admission lease before entering S for publishing construction. Session close fences node writers outside S, closes page writers under an admission lease and S, then drops both before draining maintenance callbacks and waiting for shared completions. Reader retirement acquires notification write ownership before S and rechecks admitted work. Truncation first fences local hardening/dispatch while releasing T for callbacks, then holds locally quiescent T while acquiring shared retirement ownership; remaining resource callbacks use independent readers and need no T. The shared writer truncation boundary also protects unbound page writers. Ordinary mutation lock/unlock and count-only callers are unchanged.

Focused outcome: 294 selected tests passed, followed by the final 46-case lifetime-class rerun after strengthening its early-return guard, with no selected failures or skips. Configured auxiliary tasks ran 45 cases with two skips. The diagnostic first attempt exposed repeated-listener oracle expectations and the documented distinction between direct writer close and session abort after destructive truncation; exact counts and limitations are in `data/sirix-1-0-lifecycle-fences/resource-callback-verification.json`. Mutation and final compiled/work-budget checks remain with the outer executor.
